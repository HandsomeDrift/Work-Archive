  **Reviewer 3 — Comment 1 (Novelty/architectural contribution)**
   *The framework appears incremental, assembling established components (Graphormer, ViT, FiLM, contrastive) without new structural insights or design principles.*

  **Response.** We appreciate the reviewer’s concern and have revised the paper to **clarify and verify** the architectural contributions beyond mere integration.

  1. **Early, patient-conditioned FiLM as a first-class design.** Our key novelty is to treat laboratory biomarkers as **first-class signals** and inject them **early (pre-fusion)** into **both** branches (TS and IMG) via FiLM, so that *patient-specific context conditions feature extraction itself*, rather than being appended after fusion. This design principle is now stated up-front (Abstract/Intro) and motivates the ablations we added.
  2. **Graph-informed temporal encoder for inter-lead dependencies.** We model 12-lead ECG with a Graphormer that captures **inter-lead spatiotemporal structure** rather than treating leads as independent channels. We now articulate why this differs from standard 1D-CNN/vanilla-Transformer encoders and show controlled comparisons.
  3. **Alignment-aware deep fusion.** We combine a **symmetric contrastive objective** (to enforce cross-modal semantic coherence) with a **moderate-depth Transformer fusion** module to realize fine-grained interactions. We discuss why deeper fusion shows diminishing returns and where alignment helps most (ambiguous cases).

  **What we changed.**

  - We added **parameter- and training-budget-matched** comparisons: **Early-FiLM vs Late-Concat**, **w/ vs w/o FiLM**, **w/ vs w/o contrast**, and **Graphormer-TS vs alternative TS encoders**. These isolate the **architectural** effect from mere **modality** gains and confirm that early conditioning, graph-informed TS modeling, and alignment each contribute **non-redundant** improvements (rev. ms., Sec. IV-E; Fig./Table refs).
  - We quantified cross-modal alignment beyond t-SNE using **paired TS–IMG distances** and **Alignment@k**, showing significantly weaker alignment for misclassified samples and consistent gains from the contrastive objective (rev. ms., Sec. IV-F).
  - We streamlined **claims** to reflect that the largest gains occur when **laboratory features are available** (clinical cohort), while on public datasets (no labs) our method remains competitive but not uniformly superior (rev. ms., Sec. II & VI).
  - We strengthened **Related Work** and added the **original citations** for Graphormer, FiLM, and multimodal ECG studies, clarifying our positioning and differences (rev. ms., Sec. II).

  We hope these clarifications and **controlled ablations** make the **design principles and independent contributions** of GFM-MIP explicit and verifiable.
